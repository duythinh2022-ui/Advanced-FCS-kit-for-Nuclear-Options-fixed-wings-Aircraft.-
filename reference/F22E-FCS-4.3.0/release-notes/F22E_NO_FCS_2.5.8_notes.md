# F-22E FCS 2.5.8

Base: **2.5.7**. Your call of 31 Oct 11:27: slow the *commanded roll rate*, not the move's acceleration and braking. The pace governor gets a **rate mode** (`RollPaceRateMode`, new, default true) that does exactly that, and the pitch law gets a cap for the thing that made the 37 deg spike at the end of the 11:27 log. Bench on the negative-AoA-calibrated plant, not flown.

## What the 11:27 log showed (2.5.7, level 3, margins 40/50/50 = 15/20/20)
- **Reversals at moderate AoA are slow, as you said.** Stick-reversal to body-roll zero crossing, 17 reversals: 0-15 deg AoA 0.32 s mean (max 0.45); 15-22 deg 0.55 (0.88); 22-32 deg 0.76 (1.30). 2.5.2 at level 3 crossed in 0.11-0.18 s at every AoA (with 7.5-14.2 deg of sideslip at 15-32 deg AoA); 2.5.2 at level 1 took 0.43 s with 3-8.5. So 2.5.7 was slower than both, with 3.5-7.2 deg. The window held 50-180 deg/s off the target during these (`paceHold`), and the open-loop cap ran the brake at the yaw's authority - that pacing was the design of 2.5.3, and it is what you did not want. On the bench the same shows as reversals at 15-29 deg AoA that do not reach half the opposite rate within 1.3 s.
- **The end spike (203-205 s, 36.9 deg).** Full push from a 71 deg/s yaw rotation at 63 deg AoA, 100 m/s, stick centered (`pSTarget` 14 -> 0, roll 30, rudders pinned). As the AoA swept 30 -> 0 in 0.5 s the yaw rate was still 45 deg/s; at zero AoA the coordinated roll wants no body yaw at all, so that was 45 deg/s of sideslip rate until the rudders (then at 40-70 deg/s^2) killed it: 37 deg. The pitch cap let go at 203.4 s because it was sized from the velocity-vector roll (|p cos + r sin|, 25 deg/s at 23 deg AoA), which shrinks with the AoA while the body yaw does not. Nothing in the lateral law looked at the yaw that would be left over at zero.

## Changes
1. **Rate mode** (`RollPaceRateMode` true; with `RollPaceGovernor` at `RollGovernorReplaces` >= 2). The window and the open-loop acceleration cap are off; entries, brakes and reversals run at the tuned `MaxRollAccel` and braking. The roll-rate target is capped at the rate whose *full stick reversal* the yaw can follow within the sideslip margin: the reversal swings the yaw demand by 2 x pS x sin AoA; the rudders first cross from where they are to the other stop (their slew, from the effector) and then pull at their one-side yaw (tables; past the rudder fade the allocator's yaw authority); the yaw-rate error that accumulates meanwhile, x cos AoA, is the sideslip. Solved each tick for the rate that lands on the margin (`RollStopSideslip` / `50` / `65` by AoA, your clamped 15/20/20). `PaceRateK` 1 = the kinematic estimate, no credit for the airframe's own directional stiffness; `PaceRateFloor` 10 deg/s. This is the 2.4.17/2.5.0 margin cap's idea with the lag from the rudders' crossing and yaw tables instead of a fitted 0.13-0.33 s - that fit was made under level 1's paced brakes and under-read at level 3's 720 deg/s^2.
   - **The margin is the knob again.** In pace mode it did not matter; here it buys roll rate at moderate AoA and pays in sideslip. Bench reversal sweep (27 cases, 450-700 km/h, 10-29 deg AoA), worst / mean reversal sideslip, mean roll rate, mean time from +50 % to -50 % of the roll: margin 15 (your setting) 5.8 / 3.9, 52 deg/s, 0.39 s; margin 8: 4.6 / 2.6, 34, 0.28; margin 5: 3.9 / 1.9, 24, 0.15. 2.5.7 pacing on the same cases: 5.1 / 3.1, 59 deg/s, reversal not completed in 1.3 s in 6 of 9 moderate-AoA cases. No pacing at all (the 720 brake, nothing held): 10.9 / 8.0, 83 deg/s, 0.32 s. The bench lands at about a third of the margin because the airframe's yaw stiffness helps (`PaceRateK` 0.5 would land at 13 / 7.6 with 74 deg/s - not worth it); the game's sideslip reads ~1.5x the bench's at these AoAs, so expect roughly half the margin in flight at the worst reversal.
   - Cost: roll rate at low AoA and high IAS where pacing cost nothing (700 km/h, 10 deg AoA: 116 deg/s against 163, sideslip 2.7 against 4.0). Rolling pulls 250-400 km/h: 4.8 / 6.8 / 6.0 against pacing's 3.4 / 5.1 / 3.9. Zero crossings under a roll (push + roll 200 km/h 10.5 vs 3.5, pull-then-push-over 350 4.9 vs 2.7, pull-through 450 8.8 vs 6.1): the window's "never a roll against the one in progress" is gone with the window; the AoA-rate caps and the guard carry these. All inside the margin. `paceLead` now logs the rate cap, `paceHold` what it took off the target.
   - `RollPaceRateMode` false = 2.5.7 exactly (window + acceleration cap). A/B in flight.
2. **Shed cap** (pitch law, `RollGovernorPitch`, every level >= 1): a sweep toward zero AoA may not get there sooner than the yaw can shed the yaw rate that exceeds the commanded roll's share (|r| - pS_ref |sin AoA|, less 5 deg/s for what the loop settles in a few ticks): AoA-rate cap = `SweepShedK` 0.6 x |AoA| x yaw authority / excess. Only the sweep toward zero (nose-down at positive AoA, nose-up at negative); past zero the excess is on the other side's account and the 2.1.8 sweep limit stands. A post-stall rotation with the stick centered is all excess, so the 203 s push would have been held to ~25-35 deg/s of AoA rate until the rotation was gone: rotation first, then pitch. Bench replay of 201.5-206 s (pilot's sticks, warm start): yaw at the zero crossing 26 -> 2 deg/s, sideslip 20.1 -> 19.3 - the bench does not reproduce the game's 37 (it makes 20 with the cap off, its yaw at 35-45 deg AoA is the known weak spot), so: mechanism verified on the bench, size not. On the regression crossings it helps in both modes: pace-mode pull-through 350 18.2 -> 6.0; rate-mode push + roll 200 15.6 -> 10.5, push + roll 300 7.8 -> 4.2, pull-then-push-over 350 7.6 -> 4.9, pull-through 350 9.4 -> 6.1. Variants that let a sideslip budget through at zero or released the cap below 8 deg AoA did nothing for the replay and made the crossings worse (a cap that lets go near zero steps the pitch rate while the roll is still in progress - the 2.5.3 lesson again).
