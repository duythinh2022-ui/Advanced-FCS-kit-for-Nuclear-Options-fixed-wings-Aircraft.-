## What 4.2.0 did (your 16:08 log, MPO)
- The lateral law commands a velocity-vector roll. On the body that is roll = rate × cos AoA, yaw = rate × sin AoA.
- Past 100 deg AoA (released below 80) `ReversedFlowRollSense` flipped the whole command, so body roll followed the stick. But it flipped the yaw with it.
- Between 90 and ~135 deg the yaw is the bigger part, so the nose went against the stick:

| Log time | AoA | Input | 4.2.0 command | Nose went |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 97.7-98.7 s | 100-102° | full left pedal | roll −5, yaw **+25** deg/s | right |
| 180.0-181.0 s | 134° | full right stick | roll +35, yaw **−23** | left |
| 181.3-184.8 s | 132-102° | full left stick | roll −20, yaw **+35** | right |

- At 100° the flip also reversed the command in one step with the stick held (bench: target +8.2 → −1.9 deg/s in 0.4 s).

## Change (`ReversedFlowNoseAxis`, dev, on)
- **One axis, blended on AoA, mirrored for reversed flight.** The body roll share is |cos AoA| instead of cos AoA. Yaw stays sin AoA.
  - At 90° it is pure yaw, at 135° half and half, at 180° pure roll.
  - Left stick or left pedal = nose left and roll left (positive AoA).
  - It is forward flight mirrored about the wings: at 135° the jet answers like it does at 45°.
- **No flip any more.** |cos| is continuous through 90°, so nothing reverses there. `ReversedFlowRollSense` still has to be on (it is).
- **Forward flight is untouched:** below 90° AoA |cos| = cos.
- **The pace window and the capacity estimator** use the same axis, so they don't fight it.
- **Sideslip hold:**
  - Flying backwards, that axis is not the flight path (90° off it at 135°), so turning the nose builds sideslip. Physics, not a bug.
  - **At speed the hold stays on**, as in 4.2.0. There the airframe weathervanes away from the flight path, and the hold is what brings the sideslip back (see the bench).
  - **In a slow tail slide** (below 20 m/s TAS, full hold from 40 m/s) the hold steps aside while you turn the nose. "Turning" = stick or pedal in, or the rotation still running. Faded in from 90 to 110° AoA. It comes back once you release and the rotation has stopped.
- **Negative AoA (−90 to −180):** the mirror gives what forward flight gives at negative AoA. Roll follows the stick, and the small yaw part goes the other way (like a push-roll). Near −180 it is all roll. If you want the nose to follow the stick there too, say so. It would need a smooth blend through −90, or the rotation would reverse there.
- **New telemetry column:** `revSlipHold` (the hold's share, 1 = full), at the end of the main columns.
- **Log line:** "reversed flow (flying backwards) - stick and pedals turn the nose the way they point."

| Case | AoA, IAS | Input | Body roll p (deg/s) | Body yaw r (deg/s) | Peak sideslip |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 113-124°, 113 km/h | right stick | 1.7 → 0.3 | **−3.9 → +2.8** | 2.1 → 3.3° |
| B | 132-138°, 155 km/h | left stick | −3.6 → −11.4 | **+6.9 → −6.9** | 3.7 → 16.2° |
| C | 113-124°, 113 km/h | left pedal | −1.8 → −0.3 | **+3.9 → −2.5** | 1.8 → 3.3° |

| E | 84 → 103°, 82 km/h | right stick | −0.9 → −0.9 | 3.1 → 3.2 | 2.4 → 2.3° |

- In E the 4.2.0 target fell 8.2 → 1.1 → −1.9 deg/s as the AoA passed 100°, with the stick still held. 4.3.0 holds 8.3 straight through.
- The commands follow the stick in every case. The body rates in the table are the jet's own: at 82-155 km/h flying backwards the surfaces have little authority.
- **Curves (case B):**
  - the target ramps smoothly, one slope change;
  - the rates have two slope changes, the rise and the run-out after the release;
  - no hitches or reversals during the input.
- **Hold on or off at speed (case B):**
  - Same body rates with the hold on (p −11.4 / r −6.9) or off (−11.4 / −7.4).
  - Peak sideslip 16.2° on against 24.3° off.
  - With it off after the release too, the jet hung at 27° of sideslip for over 2 s with every surface at its stop. That's why the hold stays on at speed.
